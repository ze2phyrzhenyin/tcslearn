\section*{Instructions}
Every probability solution must define the sample space, events, and random variables.

\begin{enumerate}[label=\textbf{D5.\arabic*.}]
\item Roll two fair dice. Compute the probability that the sum is 7 given that the first die is 3.
\item Give two independent events in two coin flips, and prove independence.
\item Give two disjoint nonzero-probability events and prove they are not independent.
\item Let $X$ be the number of heads in $n$ fair coin flips. Compute $\mathbb{E}[X]$.
\item In balls into bins, $m$ balls independently choose among $n$ bins. Compute expected load of bin 1.
\item Let $X,Y$ be random variables on the same space. Prove linearity of expectation for $X+Y$.
\item Use indicator variables to compute expected number of empty bins after throwing $m$ balls into $n$ bins.
\item Use indicators to compute expected number of fixed points of a random permutation of $n$ elements.
\item Use union bound for the probability that at least one of $m$ tests fails, if each fails with probability at most $\delta/m$.
\item Use union bound to bound collision probability among $k$ independently sampled values from $\{1,\dots,N\}$.
\item Apply Markov to a nonnegative random variable with expectation $\mu$.
\item Apply Chebyshev to the average of independent Bernoulli variables.
\item Use Hoeffding to bound empirical mean deviation for $n$ samples in $[0,1]$.
\item Learning preview: explain why empirical risk is a random variable when the training sample is random.
\end{enumerate}
